Time versus drawdown

One pair per line: elapsed time in minutes, then drawdown in metres. Comma, semicolon, tab or space are all accepted.

Method

Cooper-Jacob fits a straight line to drawdown against the logarithm of time by least squares, T = 2.303 Q / (4 pi ds) and S = 2.25 T t0 / r squared, where ds is the drawdown per log cycle and t0 the intercept of zero drawdown. Theis fits the full well function W(u), u = r squared S / (4 T t), by a Levenberg-Marquardt search in log T and log S started from the Cooper-Jacob estimate. Both assume a confined, homogeneous, isotropic aquifer of infinite extent, a fully penetrating well and a constant discharge.

Ground elevation

The ground surface elevation Z is read from the swisstopo digital elevation model, so the hydraulic head is H = Z minus the measured depth to the water table. A value typed by hand is kept and marked as manual.

Observation points

Method

Every point carries a ground elevation Z, read from the swisstopo digital elevation model, and a measured depth to the water table d, so the hydraulic head is H = Z - d. The head field is interpolated on a regular grid, either by inverse distance weighting with the chosen power or by linear interpolation inside a Delaunay triangulation of the points, and the equipotential lines are traced through that grid by marching squares. The regional gradient is the least squares plane fitted to the heads: its steepest descent gives the hydraulic gradient i and the flow direction, quoted as an azimuth from true north. Local vectors are the gradient of the interpolated grid. When a hydraulic conductivity and an effective porosity are given, the Darcy velocity q = K i and the mean pore velocity v = q / ne are printed as well. A single continuous isotropic aquifer is assumed, with all heads measured in that aquifer and at the same time.
